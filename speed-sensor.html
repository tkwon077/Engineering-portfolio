Speed Sensor

Terra Kwon, Ryu Pastor, Steven Damboise, Markus Fechner
GE1501 • Fall semester 2026
Overview

Pre speed sensor tasks

In our team of four, we each took on a task of practicing with a different component that was used to help build the speed sensor at the end: a HC-SR04 distance sensor, obstacle avoidance module, Buzzer + S8050 transistor, and I2C LCD 1602. At the very end, we combined what we each learned to complete the speed sensor.

Goals

What the speed sensor does

  • Measures the distance to a wall again and again
  • Works out the board’s speed from how fast that distance changes
  • Turns on a green LED at or below our set speed of 10 cm/s
  • Turns on a yellow LED when the board goes faster than that
  • Turns on a red LED when the obstacle sensor activates
  • Displays the distance and speed on the LCD as the board moves
How It Works

How each component factors in

Step 01

Measuring distance

The HC-SR04 sends out a pulse of sound and times its echo. The echo time tells the Pico how far away the wall is.

Step 02

Working out speed

The Pico measures the distance several times a second. Speed is the change in distance divided by the time between readings.

Step 03

Lighting the LEDs

The green LED indicates that it’s at or below the set speed and the yellow LED means faster. When the obstacle avoidance module turns on, the red LED turns on.

Step 04

Showing it on the LCD

The LCD’s top line shows the distance, and its bottom line shows the speed, updating with every reading.
